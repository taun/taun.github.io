Creo is Latin for "I create." Fitting. [Dan Gelbart](/n/dan-gelbart/) and [Ken Spencer](/n/ken-spencer/) founded it in 1983 in Burnaby, BC. Early products included an optical tape recorder that stored about a terabyte on a single reel of 35 mm optical tape, and laser photoplotters.

The big one was **thermal imaging**. Imaging printing plates with heat instead of light. Creo unveiled its first thermal computer-to-plate (CTP) system, the Trendsetter, at DRUPA in 1995. By 1999 it had shipped more than 1,400 imaging systems.

Then it grew fast. An IPO on the Toronto Stock Exchange and NASDAQ in 1999. In 2000 Creo bought Scitex's digital prepress business for about US$537M in stock and became [CreoScitex](/n/creo-scitex/), with about 4,200 people. It became Creo Inc. in 2002. Kodak bought it in 2005 for about US$954M in cash, and it lives on inside Kodak's print business.[^claude]
