Outdoors, finding someone is easy. GPS and a cell signal and you're done. Inside a big hotel or office building, not so much. Deep inside, "cellular communications are spotty at best and often nonexistent." Wi-Fi can fill the gap, but Wi-Fi devices are power hungry and expensive to put everywhere in a large building. And a lot of existing systems have a single point of failure that can take the whole network down.

So we went the other way. Small, cheap, **low-energy Bluetooth** devices, each at a known location, spread throughout the building.

1. A worker presses a button, or a sensor detects a condition, on a mobile device.
2. The mobile device sends the alert over Bluetooth to the nearest stationary device.
3. The stationary devices relay it hop to hop across a **Bluetooth mesh** to a gateway.
4. The gateway passes it to a server, which knows where that first stationary device is and raises the alert.
5. The alert shows up on a display, on the building's floor plan, right where it started.

This was the core technology behind ROAR's [AlwaysOn](/n/roar/) worker safety service.[^claude]
