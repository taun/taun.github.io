## About

[Alex](https://www.alexhutchinson.net/about) is a Canadian science journalist, physicist, and distance runner.

Alex presents over 200 years of research backed evidence about what affects human endurance. Stories of electrically activated frog legs. Self inflicted muscles biopsies. The impact of taking pain killers to remove the self regulation of pain (summary - don’t do it). And a whole lot more.

## I learned

The modern consensus seems to be that mostly endurance or the counter to endurance, fatigue, is regulated by the brain. Not the muscles. If the brain is hot, it will reduce the available level of effort to keep from overheating. If the brain is tired, it will reduce the level of effort available to your muscles. So to perform at your best you need to be at a comfortable temperature and well rested.

Large sections, of the book, were devoted to the body’s various types of fuel. All the point, counterpoint research on the importance of carbs vs fat throughout the ages. The current research points to both being important but during different stages of a long sustained effort. Like a marathon. Which I just happened to be training for while reading _Endure_. The body needs to be able to burn both. But if you always focus on carbs, your body doesn’t learn how to burn fat efficiently. That’s really important if during a long effort, your body runs out of carbs and it needs fuel. What’s left is your natural fats. They’re your reserve. But if you don’t have any because you refuse to eat fats, or you have never trained your body to use fats when you run out of carbs, then you **bonk**. You hit the wall. 

I was someone who historically had avoided fats. I used to call myself a “carb based life form”. What I didn’t realize is that this was part of why I was never any good at steady state endurance running. I could even “sprint, walk, sprint” faster than I could steady state run. It turns out, I had only ever trained my fast fuel supply, not my slow and steady carb and fat supply.

## I changed

Two months before my marathon, thanks to the studies cited in the _Endurance_, I started eating a lot more fat. Olive oil on everything. Lots of cheese. Full fat milk and yogurt.I realized that when I started fading after 20 minutes of running, it was because I was running out of fuel. So I focused on steadier refueling throughout my runs.

For my long runs, I replaced fancy carb based running gels with a small reusable ziploc bag full of home made trail mix, which had a combination of simple carbs (raisins), complex carbs, protein, and fats (nuts, chocolate). I would constantly graze from this bag as I ran. My body was getting a steady stream of 3 different kinds of fuel that were going to be consumed at different rates.

## Result

My runs became easier. I had more energy throughout the run without the useful fade I had been experiencing before the change. I felt like I had a lot more energy throughout the long runs (10+ miles). I’m slow, my long runs would take 2-4 hours. According to the studies presented by Endurance, that’s exactly the kind of effort where fat burning can really help. Slow and steady long duration efforts. I also felt less fatigue after the run.

The media focus on the fueling used by elite distance runners ignores the fact that us normal to slow runners have very different fuel needs. The elite run a marathon at a sprint for 2 hours. Carbs works for them. Lots of carbs. Lots and lots of carbs. Us normies run less than half the speed for at least twice as long. A little fat is good for us.

The most amazing and unexpected change from adding fat to my diet was my cold tolerance. My whole life, I worshipped heat and hated the cold because my body didn’t generate enough heat on its own. My friends were always amazed that I could find a 70 degree day cold. Almost painfully cold. Eating a lot more fat changed that. I now find a mid 60’s degree day balmy. It’s wonderful. Especially since where I live has a lot of 60 degree days. I used to always want to move to a hotter climate. Not anymore. Thanks fat! And Thanks _Endurance_. 

By the way, I finished that Marathon. I still felt fresh and fueled by the end of it. No bonking.
