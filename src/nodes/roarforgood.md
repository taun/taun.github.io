ROAR for Good started with a safety problem, not a gadget. [Yasmine Mustafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasmine_Mustafa) spent six months traveling through Spanish speaking countries and kept hearing from women who had been assaulted or harassed. When she got home to Philadelphia, she learned a neighbor had been beaten and raped. She and her team set out to build something that could help someone during an attack without being used against them. ROAR for Good launched in 2015, with co-founders Rich Nelson and Mahmoud Odeh.[^claude]

## Athena

The result was **Athena**. A stylish wearable panic button, in silver or rose gold. Press it and it sounds a loud alarm and sends your location to the people you chose, through the ROAR iOS or Android app over Bluetooth Low Energy. It launched with a heavily oversubscribed Indiegogo campaign, shipped in 2017, and sold for about $100.[^claude]

It was a hard market. Wearables stopped growing. Similar devices showed up. Athena was competing with the Apple Watch and other alert services for outdoor tracking, and it was too expensive. Customer acquisition costs tripled. In November 2018, ROAR for Good stopped selling Athena to consumers. The devices already out there kept working.[^claude]

That wasn't the end. The same team, the same BLE button experience, and a new idea became [ROAR](/n/roar/).
