What if you could grow lettuce in a warehouse next to the city that eats it? No pesticides. No weather. No cross-country trucking.

That was the bet behind Bowery Farming. Irving Fain founded it in New York in 2015, after co-founding CrowdTwist (later bought by Oracle). Bowery grew leafy greens and herbs in stacked indoor vertical farms in Kearny NJ, Maryland, and Bethlehem PA. You could buy them at Whole Foods, Walmart, Giant, Albertsons, Amazon Fresh, and more.[^claude]

## The farm is the computer

The farms were run by software. **BoweryOS** tied together sensors, computer vision, robotics, and machine learning to decide what each crop needed and when it needed it. In 2022 Bowery bought the robotics company Traptic to push the automation further.

Investors were all in. A $300M+ Series C in 2021, led by Fidelity, put Bowery at a $2.3B valuation. Total funding estimates run from about $470M to over $700M, depending on who's counting.[^claude]

## The end

High capital and energy costs, plus plant disease that took out crops across several farms, caught up with it. Bowery shut down in November 2024, after it couldn't raise more money or find a buyer.[^claude]
